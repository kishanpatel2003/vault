## Background

Prediction markets are platforms where users trade binary contracts tied to the outcome of real-world events (elections, sports, economics, geopolitics). Legally, these contracts are classified as swaps or event-based binary options under the Commodity Exchange Act (CEA) and fall under CFTC jurisdiction when traded on a federally registered Designated Contract Market (DCM). The two major U.S.-facing prediction market platforms are **Polymarket** (blockchain-based, re-entering the U.S. market via CFTC approval) and **Kalshi** (a CFTC-registered DCM, fully operational). Research was conducted across primary government sources, legal analysis from large law firms, and trade press covering regulatory developments from 2022 through early 2026.

## Key Findings

### CFTC Jurisdiction & Enforcement History

- **2022 CFTC Enforcement Action — Polymarket:** On January 3, 2022, the CFTC ordered Blockratize, Inc. d/b/a Polymarket to pay a **$1.4 million civil monetary penalty** for operating an unregistered, off-exchange facility for event-based binary options contracts (offering 900+ event markets via smart contracts on a blockchain) without designation as a DCM or registration as a SEF. Polymarket was required to wind down all non-compliant markets. Source: [CFTC Press Release 8478-22, cftc.gov](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8478-22)

- **November 2025 — Polymarket Receives Amended Order of Designation:** After restructuring, Polymarket received CFTC approval via an Amended Order of Designation to operate an intermediated trading platform subject to full DCM requirements—including enhanced surveillance systems, market supervision, clearing procedures, and Part-16 regulatory reporting. The structure enables trading through FCMs and traditional market infrastructure. Source: [Polymarket Press Release, PRNewswire, Nov 25, 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/polymarket-receives-cftc-approval-of-amended-order-of-designation-enabling-intermediated-us-market-access-302625833.html)

- **December 2025 — No-Action Letters:** The CFTC issued no-action letters to the operators of Polymarket, PredictIt, Gemini, and LedgerX/MIAX, relieving them of certain swap-related recordkeeping requirements and SDR reporting obligations for binary option transactions—provided contracts are fully collateralized and data published on-platform. Source: [CoinDesk, Dec 11, 2025](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2025/12/11/cftc-gives-no-action-leeway-to-polymarket-gemini-predictit-ledgerx-over-data-rules)

### CFTC Policy Shift Under Chairman Selig (2026)

- **January 2026 — Proposed Ban Withdrawn:** CFTC Chairman Michael S. Selig (sworn in December 2025) announced withdrawal of the 2024 proposed rule that would have **prohibited political and sports-related event contracts**, and separately withdrew a 2025 staff advisory cautioning registrants about sports-related contracts. Selig directed staff to draft new rules establishing "clear standards" for prediction markets and framed the CFTC as the "preeminent and exclusive" federal regulator of the space. Source: [Corporate Compliance Insights / Troutman Pepper Locke, Jan 30, 2026](https://www.corporatecomplianceinsights.com/cftc-withdraws-proposed-rule-prediction-markets/)

- **Insider Trading Gap:** Selig's remarks did not address how the CFTC would handle potential insider trading scenarios—e.g., a $32,000 Polymarket bet placed hours before the U.S. capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro yielded an estimated $400,000 profit, drawing public allegations of information asymmetry. The CFTC's position on such conduct remains formally unresolved. Source: [Corporate Compliance Insights, Jan 30, 2026](https://www.corporatecomplianceinsights.com/cftc-withdraws-proposed-rule-prediction-markets/)

- **CFTC Drops D.C. Circuit Appeal on Kalshi Political Contracts (May 2025):** The CFTC voluntarily dismissed its appeal of the district court ruling that political event contracts (congressional control markets) are permissible under the CEA. The pro-Kalshi district court decision now stands unchallenged at the appellate level, strengthening the legal foundation for political event contracts. Source: [Holland & Knight, May 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/new-jersey-federal-court-sides-with-kalshi-over-prediction-market)

### State-Level Regulatory Conflict

- **State Cease-and-Desist Wave (2025):** Following Kalshi's launch of sports-related event contracts in late 2024, multiple state gaming regulators issued cease-and-desist letters asserting that Kalshi's contracts constitute illegal gambling under state law. Nevada's Gaming Control Board moved first; New Jersey, Maryland, Ohio, Connecticut, New York, and Tennessee followed. Source: [iGaming Business, Dec 29, 2025](https://igamingbusiness.com/innovation/2025-prediction-markets-gaming-industry-recap/)

- **Circuit Split — Federal Courts Divided:** Courts in multiple jurisdictions have reached conflicting conclusions on whether the CEA's grant of exclusive CFTC jurisdiction preempts state gaming law:
  - **Nevada (Apr 2025):** Preliminary injunction for Kalshi — CEA field preemption likely applies to DCM transactions. (Injunction later dissolved in Dec 2025 after court found certain sports contracts "closely resemble" traditional sportsbook bets.)
  - **New Jersey (Apr 28, 2025):** Preliminary injunction granted for Kalshi — "Kalshi's sports-related event contracts fall within the CFTC's exclusive jurisdiction." (Appealed to Third Circuit.)
  - **Maryland (Aug 2025):** Preliminary injunction *denied* — strong presumption against federal preemption of state gambling power; compliance with both federal and state regimes is possible; Kalshi could obtain a Maryland gaming license. (Appealed to Fourth Circuit.)
  - **Tennessee, Ohio, Connecticut, New York:** Courts issued temporary restraining orders or paused enforcement while preliminary injunction motions are pending as of early 2026.
  Source: [Epstein Becker Green / Commercial Litigation Update](https://www.commerciallitigationupdate.com/prediction-markets-v-state-gaming-laws-the-kalshi-litigation-gamble); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/place-your-bets-us-district-courts-are-split-whether-commodity-exchange-act)

- **Federal Regulatory Model vs. State Gambling Licensing:** Sports event contracts account for more than **90% of Kalshi's platform activity**. States argue this functional equivalence with sports betting requires state licensing compliance regardless of the federal DCM designation. CFTC Chairman Selig has signaled the agency may directly intervene in pending federal cases to assert exclusive federal jurisdiction, which legal analysts note could shift case outcomes significantly. Source: [Corporate Compliance Insights / Troutman Pepper, Jan 2026](https://www.corporatecomplianceinsights.com/cftc-withdraws-proposed-rule-prediction-markets/)

## Source Tensions

**Federal preemption — unresolved split:** The most significant tension is the direct contradiction between the Nevada/New Jersey courts (favoring field preemption) and the Maryland court (finding no clear congressional intent to displace state gambling authority). The Nevada injunction was later dissolved even by the initially favorable court, illustrating how courts are shifting. No appellate court has issued a binding ruling on this question as of March 2026. Resolution likely requires either Supreme Court review or new legislation.

**CFTC's own policy inconsistency over time:** The CFTC's trajectory has been non-linear—it imposed the 2022 enforcement action, then proposed banning political/sports contracts in 2024, then dropped its own appeal of the pro-Kalshi ruling in May 2025, then approved Polymarket's re-entry in November 2025, then withdrew the proposed ban entirely in January 2026. Sources are consistent in characterizing this as a meaningful pivot, but observers note the new CFTC rulemaking has not yet been published; "clear standards" remain forthcoming.

**Insider trading / market manipulation:** Multiple secondary sources note that CFTC's existing anti-fraud and anti-manipulation authority *applies* to prediction markets, but how it interacts with information advantages unique to this asset class (e.g., geopolitical actors betting on events they have advance knowledge of) is not yet operationalized in guidance. This gap is flagged by legal analysts but has not produced any formal enforcement action as of the research date.

**State variation:** Sources are consistent that Nevada, New Jersey, Maryland, Tennessee, Ohio, Connecticut, and New York have taken active enforcement postures. There is no reporting of proactive state enforcement in states without significant gaming regulatory infrastructure, suggesting geographic patchwork even among states nominally opposed to unlicensed prediction market activity.
